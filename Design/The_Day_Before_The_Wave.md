# The Day Before the Wave — one story option

**Not locked** (2026-10-31): the user is still exploring other story types. This is one saved option. If it is

## Core beats (user's idea — keep)

1. A locked door whose **code comes from the Fish API**.
2. Behind it, the player **builds a safe shelter by completing tasks** before the wave arrives.
3. When the shelter is finished, **the wave comes and the whole village runs to the shelter**.
4. In the panic, **people push the heroine's father, and he dies in the wave**.

## Logline

Every evening the sea swallows your village, and every morning you wake up on the same day. Above the
village stands the one place that can save everyone: an old stone fort on the hill, locked for 60 years.
You have until sunset.

## Characters

- **Hiru** (14): the heroine. Her mother died when she was small; it has always been just her and Thaththa.
- **Nimal** ("Thaththa", her father): boat builder, sings badly, makes terrible jokes, loves her completely.
  When she first shows him the fort he says: *"If people panic, they'll crush each other at this door.
  Whatever happens, you close it in time. Promise me."* She promises, laughing. (Pays off at the climax.)
- **Siripala Mudalali**: owner of the village shop, rich and stubborn, adores his grandson **Kasun**.
  Hiru has to win him over to get food for the shelter. He is the one who pushes Nimal.
- **Seela Aachchi**: an old blind woman in a wheelchair who knows the legend of the Tide Bell.
- **Mr. Perera**: the village headman. Doesn't believe a 14-year-old.

## The loop: the Tide Bell

- On the first day the wave kills everyone. As it hits, Hiru grabs the rope of the old **Tide Bell** in the
  temple on the headland, and its sound turns the day back to dawn.
- Aachchi's legend: *"The bell gives the sea's children one more day, but it cracks a little each time."*
  The crack grows every loop, so the loops are limited. When it runs right through, Hiru knows:
  **this is the last day.**
- Each new day *"the sea changes small things"*. This is why the Fish API pictures (and so the codes)
  are different every loop: the randomness is part of the story.
- Things she built reset each morning, but **trust carries over as déjà vu**: *"Strange… I dreamt you
  would ask me that."* Each loop, people help sooner, so she gets further.

## Fish API — present, not central

- **The fort door**: a heavy steel door with a two-dial lock (a fish dial and a chest dial). The code is hidden
  in the old fort keeper's mural in the temple, which is an API picture painted on plaster. A new picture
  every loop means a new code every loop.
- **A few smaller locks** use the same idea: the fort storeroom, the generator box, the old radio.
- **The final twist (subversion):** at the climax the sea pulls back, and real fish and old treasure chests lie
  on the wet sand, exactly like the API pictures. The familiar counting question and timer appear one last
  time, *"How many fish?"*, and **the only right answer is to turn away and run**, shouting at everyone to
  follow. The game turns its own habit into the real-world tsunami lesson.

## Building the shelter (the tasks)

The tasks are chalked on a board inside the fort. Every task you finish visibly helps in the final run:

1. **Open the door**: Fish API code.
2. **Clear the path**: a fallen tree, and a broken rope bridge over the stream.
3. **Water**: fix the old well pump and carry water barrels up the hill.
4. **Light**: get the old generator running (fuel from the fishing harbour).
5. **Food**: win over Mudalali to give rice (help him first: find Kasun's lost kite, fix his truck).
6. **The warning**: repair the temple bell rope so the whole village hears it.
7. **The ones who can't run**: plan who carries Aachchi, the baby, and the injured fisherman.
8. **Make them believe**: show Mr. Perera the real warning signs: birds leaving, dogs howling, the well
   water dropping.

Between tasks: small moments with Thaththa (fixing his boat, flying a kite, eating kottu at the shop, learning
knots). In every loop the player gets to know him a little better.

## The last day (climax)

1. Late afternoon: Hiru rings the bell. Some villagers come; many don't.
2. **The sea pulls back.** Fish flap on the sand, old chests appear, and villagers run *toward* the sea. The
   counting picture appears one last time, and the right answer is to run.
3. A white line appears on the horizon. Panic. Everyone runs up the hill on the path you cleared. The bridge
   holds; the fort lights you fixed are on.
4. **At the fort steps**: chaos. Thaththa is carrying Aachchi up the steps. Mudalali, clutching Kasun, panics
   and shoves through. Thaththa loses his footing, pushes Aachchi through the doorway first, and falls.
5. Hiru is at the door controls. The water is racing up the hill. From below, Thaththa shouts:
   *"Hiru! Close the door! You promised!"* **The player has to hold the button to close it.** The game will
   wait as long as you wait, until his voice, quieter: *"Please, Duwa."*
6. Darkness. The roar of water outside. Three hundred people breathing in the dark. Then the lamps come on,
   the ones she fixed.
7. Morning. Hiru runs to the temple to ring the Tide Bell and go back. It is cracked right through and makes
   no sound.

## Epilogue (years later)

- The village has been rebuilt on higher ground. The fort is now a school and a tsunami shelter, named after
  Nimal.
- **The memorial wall** lists the people the village lost. The more tasks you finished, the fewer names there
  are, but **Thaththa's name is always there**.
- Old Mudalali comes with Kasun, now grown up, and kneels to ask forgiveness. **The player chooses** whether
  Hiru forgives him.
- Final scene: Hiru, now a teacher, tells a class of children: *"When the sea runs away, you run to the hill."*
  Kasun is one of the volunteers. At sunset on the hill she hears her father singing, off-key, in the wind.
- Possible dedication before the credits: *"For everyone the sea took on 26 December 2004."*

## Tone rules

This story is close to the real 2004 tsunami. No graphic deaths: show the wave through sound, scale and
people's faces. The focus is on courage, panic, love and forgiveness.

## Idea bank (proposals, not decided yet — 2026-10-31)

⭐ = recommended.

**Big moments**
- ⭐ **The same button**: in loop 1 you hold a button to keep hold of Thaththa's hand as you run, and you don't
  make it. On the last day, closing the door uses that same hold button.
- ⭐ **The boat with her name**: tomorrow is Hiru's 15th birthday, and Thaththa is secretly building her a small boat
  ("Don't look!"). After the wave the boat is found up on the hill, unbroken, with her name on it. Final scene:
  she sails it.
- ⭐ **His song in the dark**: Thaththa sings the same off-key song every loop. In the dark shelter a child starts
  humming it, then all 300 people join in.
- **The kite**: flying a kite together each loop. After the wave, its string is found caught in the tree by the
  memorial.
- **"You've lived this day before, haven't you?"**: on the last day Thaththa sees the cracked bell and believes her
  when nobody else does: *"If this is the last time, let's make it a good day."* One free hour together.

**Gameplay**
- **The Loop Journal**: Hiru's notebook is the only thing that survives a loop (she holds it as she rings the bell).
  It is the game's menu: tasks, codes, routines, and Thaththa's jokes she is collecting.
- **Not enough time**: one day is about 20–30 minutes. You can't finish every task in one loop, so you plan
  with what earlier loops taught you.
- **Routines**: villagers do the same things at the same times every day; learn them to plan the perfect day.
- **No game over**: about 5 loops (5 cracks). Whatever you finished by the last day decides the outcome, shown
  on the memorial wall.
- **Ripple choices**: help someone in one loop and their déjà vu changes in the next (save the village dog once,
  and on the last day it leads the children up the hill).

**More Fish API (still subtle)**
- **Boats at sea**: each morning the harbour board shows an API picture. Fish = how many boats are out today and
  must be called back by radio; chests = how many supply crates the trader's lorry brings. It's different
  every loop, so every plan is different.
- **Made for children**: the 1942 fort keeper survived a great wave as a boy. His diary says he made the lock a
  counting puzzle "so a child who pays attention can open it — grown-ups never look closely."
- **Kasun, the counting boy**: Mudalali's grandson always sits counting the temple mural and helps Hiru open the
  door. The boy who helped open the shelter is the reason his grandfather panicked.

**Characters and twists**
- ⭐ **Thirty seconds as Mudalali**: before the forgiveness choice, a short playable memory through his eyes:
  the crowd, the water, Kasun screaming, he can't see, and the push happens in a blur.
- **Kalu the dog**: a village stray who follows Hiru each loop and senses the wave first.

**Ending**
- ⭐ **The last page**: after the credits, the last page of Hiru's journal is in Thaththa's handwriting, written on the
  last day while she wasn't looking: *"Whatever happens, you saved them. I'm proud of you, Duwa."*
- **Names on the shelter wall**: that night the survivors carve their names inside the shelter. In the epilogue you can
  walk along them, and each one is someone you helped.
- **The bell recast**: the village recasts the Tide Bell, and every year Hiru's students ring it. It never turns
  back time again: "Now it's just for remembering."
